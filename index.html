A Letter to My Future Self

Your letterFor my younger self.

Sent from
age 20
✉️Grade 10

Dear 16-year-old me,

Hey, how are you? I hope you're doing well. It's me, your 20-year-old self. I know you have plenty of questions about what the future holds, and I wish I could tell you everything that will happen. However, what I can tell you is that the journey ahead will teach you more than you could ever imagine.

Looking back, I can proudly say that I have become someone who is more mature, independent, disciplined, and understanding. I have learned to appreciate the little things in life and understand that personal growth takes time. I am no longer the person who constantly worries about what others think. Instead, I have learned to focus on my own progress and take responsibility for my decisions.

Do you remember the dreams you used to have? Becoming a successful software engineer, studying at the University of Santo Tomas, and building a better future for yourself? I hope you realize how meaningful those dreams were. I have worked hard to turn those ambitions into reality, and I am proud of how far I have come. My curiosity about mathematics, science, and programming has continued to guide me toward the career I wanted.

The journey wasn't always easy. I experienced failures, disappointments, and moments when I questioned my own abilities. I also encountered situations that tested my patience and challenged my beliefs. Nevertheless, I managed to overcome those difficulties by staying persistent and trusting God's plans. I made mistakes along the way, too. Some decisions taught me that not everything deserves my attention, and that sometimes, letting go is part of growing up. I learned that I cannot control everything, but I can always control how I respond.

I am also grateful for the people who helped me reach this point. My family, friends, and teachers have contributed to my growth in ways I will never forget. Above all, I am thankful to God for giving me strength and guidance throughout my journey.

If I could give you some advice, I would tell you to never underestimate your potential. Continue learning, stay humble, and remain consistent with your goals. Don't let temporary failures define your capabilities, and never sacrifice your values just to gain recognition or approval from others.

Lastly, remember this: I wish I had understood earlier that life is not a race against other people. I don't need to become successful before everyone else; I just need to become a better version of myself every single day.

With love and gratitude,
Your 20-year-old self
